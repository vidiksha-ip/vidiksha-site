Trademark prosecution is the legal process of working with a government trademark office (such as IP India or the USPTO) to register, maintain, and defend a trademark. It covers everything from filing the initial application to overcoming office rejections and managing public oppositions. christensen-law.com +1

Key Steps in the Trademark Prosecution Process (India)

• Pre-Filing Search: Check existing records in the public database to ensure no similar or identical marks exist. iPleaders Blog +1
• Application Filing: Submit Form TM-A online or at the registry under one or more of the 45 Nice Classes (Classes 1–34 for goods, 35–45 for services). BananaIP Counsels +1
• Formalities Check & Examination: The Trademark Registry reviews the application and issues an Examination Report highlighting any objections (commonly under Section 9 for distinctiveness or Section 11 for prior conflicting marks). iPleaders Blog +1
• Response to Objections: File a formal legal reply with evidence (such as sales data or market use) within the statutory response window (typically 30 days to 1 month). Khurana And Khurana +2
• Publication in the Trademark Journal: If accepted, the mark is published for four months to allow third parties to review it. iPleaders Blog +1
• Opposition Proceedings: If a third party files an opposition (Form TM-O), the process moves to counterstatements, evidence filing, and registry hearings. iPleaders Blog +1
• Registration & Renewal: Unopposed or successful marks receive a registration certificate valid for 10 years
